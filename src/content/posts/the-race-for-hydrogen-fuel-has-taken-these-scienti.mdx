## The Details

Juan Galarza , a molecular ecologist at the University of Oulu in Finland, often took an L track of time because of these “disorienting” conditions, as he and his team combed the high Arctic during the summer of 2024. They spent the perpetual daylight obsessively staring at a deep-sea scanner’s monitor and hoping for signs of methane bubbles.

Such a signal could indicate that they had found a cold seep , a deep-sea site from which fluids or gases, like methane, escape through cracks on the seafloor. Through an energy-producing process known as chemosynthesis, microscopic life can sustain itself on these gases, thriving in the total darkness and near-freezing conditions.
