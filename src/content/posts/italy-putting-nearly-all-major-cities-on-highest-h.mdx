## What's Happening

So basically Some 19 major Italian cities - including Rome, Milan and Naples - are already on red alert, and by Monday all but two will be.

Italy putting nearly all major cities on highest heat alert Image source, Anadolu via Image caption, Italian health officials urge residents in the affected areas to avoid exposure to heat and direct sunlight and drinking plenty of fluids By Jaroslav Lukiv Published 1 August 2026 Italy is putting nearly all of its major cities on the highest "red" alert for high temperatures, as a new heatwave grips the country. Health officials say 19 cities from north to south - including the capital Rome, Milan, Venice and Naples - are already at red alert, and but two cities will be. (wild, right?)

Temperatures hit mid-30s C in many places on Saturday, and authorities are warning of risks to even healthy and active people - not just groups including the elderly, young children and people with chronic illnesses.

## The Details

The warnings come as several European nations battle devastating wildfires after a succession of heatwaves that have created favourable conditions for blazes. It says it expects only two - Reggio Calabria in the southern tip of Italys "boot" and Messina, facing it across on the island of Sicily - to be on the second-highest "amber" alert. The ministry urges residents in the affected areas to avoid exposure to heat and direct sunlight during the hottest hours between 11:00 and 18:00, stay indoors and drink plenty of fluids.

## Why This Matters

The extreme heat is being caused by a subtropical anticyclone from North Africa that is now strengthening over the Mediterranean. Meanwhile, firefighters in Greece, France, Portugal and Spain are still tackling blazes spanning thousands of hectares.
