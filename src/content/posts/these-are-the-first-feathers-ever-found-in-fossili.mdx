## The Details

Dense bristles suggest some of the feathers could repel water , possibly as well as those of modern pelicans, researchers report September 10 in Current Biology . And their central shafts are filled with air pockets, a trait that in modern birds keeps plumage lightweight yet stiff enough for flight.

“That’s something we’re seeing for the first time in the Age of the Dinosaurs,” says vertebrate paleontologist Jingmai O’Connor of the Field Museum of Natural History in Chicago. For our We summarize the week's scientific breakthroughs every Thursday.

## Why This Matters

The find also offers a clue that might explain why, among birds, there were winners and losers in the mass extinction that wrapped up the dinosaurs’ reign about 66 million years ago. The ancestors of modern birds survived the asteroid-triggered event while many other types of birds, including the group these feathers came from, died out. One of O’Connor’s colleagues unearthed the fossilized dung, or coprolite, in 2016 in Montana’s Hell Creek Formation.

Scientists and researchers are watching this development closely.
