Childhood cancer symptoms can be easily missed - heres what to look out for Image source, Family handout Image caption, Amelia Carroll was 13 when she got her cancer diagnosis last year By Camilla Horrox Health reporter Published 12 October 2026, 00:03 BST Although rare, cancer is the leading cause of death from disease among children under 15 in the UK - yet just under a third of parents say they feel confident recognising the symptoms. About 1,900 children aged 0-14 are diagnosed with cancer each year, according to Cancer Research UK. (yes, really)

Thirteen-year-old Amelia Carroll, who is now in remission, was diagnosed with acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) last year.

## The Details

Her mum Gemma recalls the initial symptoms being easy to overlook. There was the tiredness, she says, and being out of breath after mild exercise but both were put down to being unfit and just post-school fatigue.

It was only when Amelia kicked off presenting with bruises Gemma knew something wasnt right - within 10 days Amelia got her leukaemia diagnosis. What are the symptoms to look out for?

## Why This Matters

Prof Bob Phillips, a paediatric oncologist, says its key for parents to look out for clusters of symptoms that may be persisting, getting worse or bigger. Consider contacting a doctor over the following: Extreme tiredness Looking fr pale Bruises that you cant explain Lumps and bumps that are getting bigger or not going away and growing over time Cancer in children develops much more quickly than in adults - effectively because of them growing. The experts like Phillips at CCLG, the Children and Young Peoples Cancer Association, are urging people to follow a national campaign aiming to raise awareness and improve diagnosis times.

## The Bottom Line

The experts like Phillips at CCLG, the Children and Young Peoples Cancer Association, are urging people to follow a national campaign aiming to raise awareness and improve diagnosis times. It includes a rule-of-three for professionals: If a child has had three persistent symptoms, or has visited their doctor three times, or has had three weeks of unexplained symptoms consider making a referral.
