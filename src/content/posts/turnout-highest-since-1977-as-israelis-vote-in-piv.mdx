## What's Happening

Here's the thing: The elections - the first since the Hamas-led attacks on Israel and the war which followed - are widely seen as a referendum on Benjamin Netanyahu.

Turnout highest since 1977 as Israelis vote in pivotal election Image source, Reuters / Image caption, Polling Friday suggests Gadi Eisenkot (left) is in a tight race with incumbent Benjamin Netanyahu (right) By Tom Bennett Reporting from Jerusalem and Tel Aviv Published 27 October 2026, 05:45 GMT Updated 4 minutes ago Turnout in Israels election is at its highest since 1977, as Israelis vote in what many view as one of the most pivotal elections in the countrys history. Polling Friday suggests incumbent Benjamin Netanyahu is almost neck-and-neck with former military chief Gadi Eisenkot in the race to become prime minister. (yes, really)

But neither of their political blocs are projected to win the 61 seats needed to form a majority government - meaning weeks or months of political wrangling may lie ahead.

## The Details

The campaign has been shaped of democracy and security, and the election is widely being seen as a referendum on Netanyahus rule. Many leading parties complained on Tuesday morning of alleged irregularities at polling stations across the country - but Israels Central Election Committee (CEC) says there is no evidence of systematic tampering with ballot slips.

Israels proportional representation system means governments have always been coalitions - and the race to power can, and has, produced unexpected alliances. But this election looks especially tight - and lines in the sand have already been drawn.

## Why This Matters

Eisenkot, who leads the newly formed Yashar Party, is the main figure in the so-called "change bloc", a collection of parties who have ideological differences but the goal of ousting Netanyahu. Other leaders in the bloc include former prime minister Naftali Bennett and Avigdor Lieberman - both right-wingers who take hardline positions on security - and Yair Golan, a left-leaning former general.

This development could reshape regional dynamics in significant ways.
