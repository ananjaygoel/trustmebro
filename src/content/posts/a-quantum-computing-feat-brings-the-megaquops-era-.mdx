## The Details

That “megaquop” regime — as it’s called — isn’t here yet. But scientists just took a significant step toward it.

A quantum computer built Quantinuum has error rates so low that it could perform a million operations with only about five errors , researchers report in a paper posted October 19 on the company’s site and arXiv. For our We summarize the week's scientific breakthroughs every Thursday.

## Why This Matters

“These statistics are consistent with this being a good building block for that megaquop machine,” says quantum physicist David Hayes of Quantinuum, who is based in Broomfield, Colo. To reach that regime, but, such computers will need to be scaled up. Quantinuum’s computer, known as Helios, is made of electrically charged atoms, called ions, hovering over a chip.

This could have implications for future research in this area.

## Key Takeaways

- Those ions serve as the 98 quantum bits, or qubits, of the computer.
- But, like all qubits, those ions are finicky.
- Their states are delicate and can flip unintentionally and mess up calculations, like a 0 randomly switching to a 1.
