## The Details

Now, researchers have developed a way for tiny implantable devices to send electrical signals through the body’s own tissues. The approach could offer a low-power way for implants to work together , with one detecting a symptom and another delivering treatment, the team reports September 24 in Science .

Rn, getting devices to communicate through tissue comes with trade-offs. Some medical devices, such as insulin pumps linked to glucose monitors, use Bluetooth, which relies on radio signals.

## Why This Matters

## Key Takeaways

- The new network called SWANS, the Smart Wireless Autonomous Networking System, works differently.
- A wearable external hub or implant generates an electric pulse that produces a small current in the surrounding tissue.
- Tiny implants can detect those electrical signals while using almost no power when they are only listening.
- And without large batteries, those implants can be small enough to inject through a syringe.
