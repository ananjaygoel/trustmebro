## What's Happening

Listen up: The aim is to detect hard-to-diagnose abdominal cancers, including liver, ovarian and pancreatic cancer, much sooner.

Multi-cancer blood tests coming to some GP surgeries soon Image source, By Michelle Roberts Digital health editor Published 4 November 2026 New blood tests designed to check for multiple cancers in one go will be offered to NHS patients in some 200 GP surgeries in England as part of a trial. These cancers are often found only after they have spread. (let that sink in)

The tests look for proteins or fragments of DNA in the blood that may signal cancer.

## The Details

Results could help doctors decide which patients with vague symptoms need further investigations. More than 20,000 patients aged 45 and over with symptoms such as pain or persistent bloating will be eligible to take part.

Hard-to-detect cancers The government-funded trial, led of Birmingham, will assess whether the tests are accurate enough for wider NHS use. The idea of a blood test that can identify multiple cancers is not new.

## Why This Matters

A separate NHS trial of a test called Galleri just flopped to meet its main goal of reducing diagnoses of late-stage cancers, although researchers are still assessing its potential benefits. Prof Peter Johnson, NHS Englands national clinical director for cancer, dropped detecting abdomen cancer early was "notoriously difficult" because symptoms can easily be mistaken for other less serious conditions. Earlier diagnosis gives patients a much better chance of successful treatment and survival.

Medical professionals are taking note of this development.

## The Bottom Line

The new STRIDE-RCT trial , external is about detecting bowel, small bowel, stomach, oesophageal, pancreatic, liver, bile duct and gallbladder, ovarian, endometrial, kidney, bladder and prostate cancer. Patients will provide one or two tubes of blood, taken at their GP practice, alongside routine tests.
