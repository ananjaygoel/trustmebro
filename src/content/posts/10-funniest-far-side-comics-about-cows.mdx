Com account From 1980 until 1995, cartoonist Gary Larson wrote and drew the comic strip The Far Side . (wild, right?)

It was a single panel comic strip filled with non-sequitur, often surreal jokes mostly aimed at adults instead of children, which was unusual for newspaper comics.

## The Details

Also unusual was the fact that, unlike most daily comic strips, there were no recurring characters. Whereas Peanuts had its recurring gang of kids and Garfield has its cast of humans and animals, every panel of The Far Side introduced new characters and scenarios all aimed at delivering that day’s joke.

That dropped, Larson did have some themes and types of characters he’d often return to. Dinosaurs, for example, were a big one as Larson would depict them doing normal human things like eating breakfast cereal (with a free kid inside) or smoking cigarettes (the real reason they became extinct, according to Larson).

## Why This Matters

He also liked to explore the domesticity of prehistoric life through cavemen and to lampoon historical figures and moments. But if one recurring theme of Larson’s The Far Side is a standout, it’s his cows. Over the 15 years he was doing The Far Side , Larson created 4,337 different comics and he returned to cows again and again as a source of humor.
