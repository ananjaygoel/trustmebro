## What's Happening

Okay so Specialist glasses are all but sold out online ahead of the UK's most significant eclipse since 1999.

"Ive sold, like, 100 this morning," a staff member tells the BBC. (wild, right?)

But its not baked goods driving queues out of the door - users are here to buy eclipse glasses.

## The Details

They are among those hoping to catch a safe glimpse of Wednesdays solar eclipse - the UKs most significant since 1999 - but struggling to get their hands on the right gear. The experts have urged eclipse enthusiasts not to look directly at the Sun - with even brief exposure causing lasting damage - and to seek out specialist glasses instead.

But online, pairs that meet safety specifications have all but sold out. Those still available either require a hefty next-day delivery fee or simply cannot arrive on time.

## Why This Matters

Even the Royal Museums Greenwich - recommended as a reputable retailer officials - has sold out, citing an "unprecedented volume" of orders. "I Googled where can I get eclipse glasses but not through the post, because I realised I was too late," one customer waiting to buy three pairs at the bakery on Monday tells BBC Radio 4s PM programme. "People have been struggling to find them all over," says another.

This could have implications for future research in this area.

## The Bottom Line

The right glasses should have the international safety standard ISO 12312-2 printed on them, the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has advised, and the lenses should not be scratched, torn or damaged. It has also advised caution surrounding glasses being sold online, as the scramble for safe spectacles has prompted eclipse chasers to trawl socials for tips.
