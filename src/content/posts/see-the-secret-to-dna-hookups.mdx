## The Details

In a first, scientists have visualized how pieces of DNA pair up. Computer simulations further reveal that charged metal ions help twin double helices align, researchers report September 9 in Nucleic Acids Research .

The work adds insight to the process in which cells pair up chromosomes before divvying DNA into eggs or sperm. Matching the chromosomes allows them to exchange information and keeps them from going astray, much like how folding socks keeps pairs together.
