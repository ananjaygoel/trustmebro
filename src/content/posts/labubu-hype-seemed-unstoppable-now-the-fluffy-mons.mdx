## What's Happening

Breaking it down: Parent company Pop Mart's revenue has fallen as appetite for the quirky collectible monsters wanes.

Labubu hype seemed unstoppable, now the fluffy monster mania is dying Image source, Courtesy of Ensito Image caption, Ensito, with his custom-made Labubu teeth, posing with a new doll in his collection By Osmond Chia Business reporter Published 4 hours ago For years, Labubu dolls seemed unstoppable. Collectors young and old energetically snapped up "blind boxes" - sealed, mystery packages containing the quirky monsters - often paying $15 (£11) or more. (we're not making this up)

Those unable to get the characters they wanted were often prepared to pay much more on the resale market.

## The Details

The hype around the creatures convinced a mega-fan from North Carolina, who goes Ensito, to leave his tech job in 2024 to create Labubu content full-time, travelling as far as Italy and Spain to seek out the latest releases. Ensito has spent more than $50,000 (£37,000) to build his collection of more than 400 Labubus, a figure he tells the BBC hes "embarrassed to say".

He even had a row of custom Labubu-like jagged teeth made by a dentist to wear at events. But in recent months, Ensitos once-viral posts about the furry monsters have struggled.

## Why This Matters

"Those videos used to be able to hit 10,000 fr quickly but Im struggling to do that now. " Ensitos focus has shifted to other trends like Hello Kitty and squishies. Its a sign that the craze which made parent company Pop Mart worth more than US toy giants Hasbro and Mattel put together may be running out of steam.

Market watchers are paying close attention to developments like this.

## Key Takeaways

- Analysts believe the Beijing-based brand is struggling to capture excitement beyond a single viral moment.
- Pop Mart just reported that global sales in the three months to the end of September had fallen as 50% on the previous year.
- The drop was particularly steep outside mainland China, with US revenue down 70%.
- Image source, Reuters The rise and fall Pop Mart didnt invent blind boxes but its dolls certainly helped to popularise them around the world.
