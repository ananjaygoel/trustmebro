For decades, residents have had to endure rank smells wafting from industrial sites, among them a 342-acre landfill. (plot twist fr)

"When I first moved here, I could not open my window for over 10 years," says Chia-Ling Kong, an engineer for a tech firm who lives around half a mile from the dump.

## The Details

While things have got better in recent years, Kong says she and her neighbours must still contend with around "two or three hours of rough smells a day". Perhaps befitting Silicon Valley, the community is putting its hopes in technology.

The operators of New have been ordered to use drones and install monitors to systematically track and measure the chemicals responsible for rough odours. They have also been told to automate the collection of landfill gases responsible for smells.

## Why This Matters

Kong is optimistic a data-driven approach will make a difference. "Hopefully with this continuous monitoring, it makes it easier to pinpoint problems at the moment it starts. " The operators of the landfill say they are committed to operating it safely and protecting air quality.

This could have implications for future research in this area.

## Key Takeaways

- Sometimes a rubbish dump is to blame, or it might be a sewage treatment works, or a reeking factory.
- When the complaints start stacking up, resolving them is not always simple.
- Many countries have no regulations in place dictating how to manage smells.

## The Bottom Line

In the US, for example, the Environmental Protection Agency does not regulate environmental odours as a pollutant. This leaves states and local governments to take charge of the issue.
