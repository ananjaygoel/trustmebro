## The Details

Mild-mannered outside of breeding season, they star in a spectacular springtime courtship display in the wetlands of Europe, North Africa, the Middle East and Central Asia. Come spring, males perform a series of intricate dances that include sideways swimming, exuberant splashing and beating their puffed-up neck feathers with their bills to produce bubble trails.

Their exceptionally long and stiff tail feathers are part of the show, waving like flags, spreading like the fandom and slapping the water like paddles. “To witness white-headed ducks in courtship is to watch one of the avian world’s most extraordinary performances,” says Nicky Petkov , a waterfowl expert with both the Bulgarian Society for the Protection of Birds and BirdLife International, an arm of the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN).

## Why This Matters

In Spain, white-headed ducks also star in a spectacular conservation success story. Decades of draining wetlands for agriculture and development in the past century shrank habitat and tanked numbers across the ducks’ range. Hunting took a further toll on the global population, which had been estimated at some 100,000 in the 1930s.

The scientific community tends to find developments like this significant.
