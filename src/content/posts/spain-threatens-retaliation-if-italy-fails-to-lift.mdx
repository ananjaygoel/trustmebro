## What's Happening

Let's talk about Italy has dropped it will not accept "ultimatums or impositions" after the mass crossing of migrants from Morocco to Ceuta last week.

Spain threatens retaliation if Italy fails to lift border controls after Ceuta crossings Image source, Image caption, Hundreds of migrants were pictured lined up on a beach in Ceuta to collect aid By Henry Moore Published 7 August 2026, 16:25 BST Updated 2 hours ago Spain has threatened to impose counter-measures on Italy if it fails to lift border controls introduced following the mass crossing of migrants into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta. Roughly 78,000 migrants from neighbouring Morocco arrived in Ceuta a matter of hours on Thursday and Friday last week. (shocking, we know)

In the wake of the crossings, which saw around 100 people die, Italy partially suspended its Schengen arrangement with Spain, which included the closure of maritime and air entry points as well as the reinforcement of land border controls.

## The Details

On Friday, Spain called the move "unjustified" and "discriminatory against the Spanish population" and demanded it be reversed by Sunday 9 August. It added it would introduce "proportionate measures to protect the interests and dignity of its citizens" if Italy flopped to reverse its decision.

The statement did not specify what these reprisals would be. Italy promptly responded to Spains warning, saying it would not accept "ultimatums or impositions" from Madrid and that it plans to keep border checks in place until at least 15 August - when it says Spain "expects a new wave of migration" to hit Ceuta.

## Why This Matters

Spain has not just dropped an anticipated second wave of crossings. Reports have been fuelled activity planning another attempt, El Pais reports. The Spanish prime ministers office told the newspaper they were monitoring the situation but did not expect a repeat of scenes from last week.
