## The Details

On Tuesday, NASA dropped a Moon Base video update offering a closer look at the progress being made on the commercial lunar terrain vehicles and robotic rovers that will enable the surface mobility needed to build humanity’s first lunar outpost. “Lunar rovers are central to our surface mobility strategy,” dropped Robert Pickle, director of the Surface Mobility Office within the agency’s Moon Base Program.

“Their performance will lay the groundwork for the mobility systems that will define the next era of exploration and ultimately determine how effectively we can build, operate, and expand the infrastructure required for long‑duration exploration. ” Learn more about NASA’s VIPER An artists concept of NASAs VIPER lunar rover on the surface of the Moon.

## Why This Matters

NASA/Daniel Rutter NASA’s VIPER (Volatiles Investigating Polar Exploration Rover) will embark on a mission to the lunar South Pole region in 2027 to venture into permanently shadowed craters looking for water and other valuable resources essential for sustainable exploration. After delivery to the lunar surface by a Blue Moon Mark 1 lander, VIPER will travel more than 18 miles (30 kilometers), collecting information about the thermal, lighting, and terrain environment in the Moon’s South Pole region.
