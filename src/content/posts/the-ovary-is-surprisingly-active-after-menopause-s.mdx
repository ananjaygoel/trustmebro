## The Details

In a woman’s reproductive years , the ovary is exceptionally dynamic, shifting form and function from day to day throughout her cycle. But after menopause, when egg supply has dwindled and hormone production has waned, the ovaries are often thought of as little more than empty sacks .

“I assumed it was just sitting there inert,” says Duncan, a reproductive biologist at Northwestern University, who was only looking at postmenopausal ovaries as part of a project to map a cellular aging process called senescence throughout the body. Instead, Duncan found that aged ovaries were anything but stagnant.

## Why This Matters

When looking at the protein composition of ovaries of postmenopausal women between the ages of 50 and 75 years old, her team found that the molecular signatures differed majorly across the decades. The oldest ovary looked nothing like a 60- or 70-year-old organ at the molecular level. “Clearly something is still happening,” says Duncan.

This is part of the broader shift happening across the tech industry right now.

## The Bottom Line

A new wave of discovery is forcing researchers to recast the ovary not as an egg factory with an expiration date, but as an organ that impacts a woman’s overall health and may shape the trajectory of aging long after the fertile years have passed. What they are only beginning to understand is whether changes to the ovary after menopause are harmful, protective, or some combination of the two, shifting over time.
