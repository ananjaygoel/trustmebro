## What's Happening

So get this: Trump is suing the BBC over an episode of Panorama that edited together two different sections of a speech he gave in 2021.

Trump ordered to answer questions under oath in BBC lawsuit Image source, Kent NISHIMURA / AFP via By Bernd Debusmann Jr White House reporter Published 22 October 2026 Updated 54 minutes ago A US judge has ordered President Donald Trump to answer questions under oath in his defamation lawsuit against the BBC by 11 December. Trump cancelled a previous deposition - or interview under oath - on 16 October, citing "matters of national importance". (yes, really)

But a lawyer representing the BBC dropped in court that Trump was campaigning in Georgia that day and had "prioritised that" over answering the questions.

## The Details

President Trumps lawsuit against the BBC is in the phase known as "discovery", where the two sides provide internal or other records that could form evidence. Charles Tobin, a lawyer for the BBC, told a court in Miami on Thursday that with two months left in the discovery period, Trump had still not completed a deposition.

US Magistrate Judge Enjoliqué Lett dropped Trump must sit for the deposition by 11 December or face sanctions - the most extreme of which could be the dismissal of the lawsuit - unless the discovery period is extended. In US law, a deposition is an out-of-court statement made under oath in response to questions, which is recorded for use in later proceedings.

## Why This Matters

Depositions carry legal weight - they are recorded verbatim by a court reporter and the transcripts can later be used as evidence in trials. They typically take place in a lawyers office without a judge physically present. In defamation cases, lawyers for both sides are normally present for depositions.
