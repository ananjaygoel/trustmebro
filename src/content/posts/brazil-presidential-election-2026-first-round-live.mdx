## Why This Matters

The first is the incumbent president, Lula, 80, a veteran of the Brazilian left who has served three terms. During his first two terms, between 2003 and 2011, he was credited with overseeing a historic period of commodity-fuelled growth and poverty reduction. Brazilian presidents are limited to two consecutive terms, so if he wins this time he will not be able to stand in 2030.
