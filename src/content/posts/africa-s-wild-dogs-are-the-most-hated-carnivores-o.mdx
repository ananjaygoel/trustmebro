## The Details

Just visible through the holes at the top of three green wooden crates is fur over rib cages, mottled black, white and gold, rising and falling. The three sleeping female African wild dogs, sisters, have traveled a long way in the past few weeks, even of their species, which can cover 30 miles a day and occupy ranges of 1,000 miles.

They arrived Karingani Game Reserve in Mozambique, courtesy of the Endangered Wildlife Trust’s African wild dog range expansion program . The project, which kicked off in 1997, has increased the world’s wild dog population 344 in 29 years.

## Why This Matters

It introduces dogs to wildlife reserves in countries where they haven’t been seen in decades—for instance, Mozambique, where the species disappeared during a civil war that lasted from 1977 to 1992. The Mozambican packs have bred so successfully that the Endangered Wildlife Trust (EWT) is now bringing young dogs to South Africa to form new, genetically diverse packs here.

This could have implications for future research in this area.
