## The Details

A self-taught astronomer, he built his own telescopes to observe Mars and Jupiter. Tombaugh wrote a letter to the Lowell Observatory in Flagstaff, Arizona, asking for a professional opinion on his celestial drawings, and the staff wrapped up up offering him a job as an assistant, searching for a hypothesized, elusive ninth planet.

On , at the observatory, he was studying photographic glass-plate images he had taken with a telescope. Using a blink comparator , he examined two plates captured days apart, and noticed a tiny dot featured on both of them but in different locations.

## Why This Matters

That dot was the small and icy Pluto. Tombaugh had found what he and observatory leadership were seeking. “It took the right person with the right amount of focus, because it was a needle in a haystack looking at dots—a dot that moved against the background of hundreds of thousands of dots,” says Kevin Schindler , the longtime historian and public information officer at the Lowell Observatory.
