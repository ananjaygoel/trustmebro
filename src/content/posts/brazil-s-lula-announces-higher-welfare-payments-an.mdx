## The Details

10 EDT Last modified on Fri 18 Sep 2026 13. 11 EDT Prefer the Guardian on Google Brazil’s president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva , has just dropped a 15% increase in the country’s main cash-transfer programme for weak families, as well as free weight-loss jabs, prompting criticism from opponents who say the moves are electorally motivated just two weeks before the presidential election.

The rise in the monthly payment, from about £98 to £113, that he just dropped on Thursday is because of take effect on 19 October, just before the likely runoff vote on 25 October. The increase is expected to benefit between 15% and 20% of voters in what promises to be one of the closest ever presidential races in Latin America’s largest country.

## Why This Matters

Lula dropped it was not fr an increase, but an adjustment in line with inflation to “protect the purchasing power of the most vulnerable families”, but his opponents have criticised its timing so close to the election.

International observers are watching how this situation develops.
