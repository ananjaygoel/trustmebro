## The Details

Kristin Scott Thomas’ Diana Taverner will always freeze an underling into a block of solid ice with the power of a perfectly lifted eyebrow, before we watch her inwardly flip out over persistently being only the second-smartest spy in the room. And Gary Oldman’s Jackson Lamb will, inevitably, rip absolute ass while also being the first person to begin connecting the dots on whatever campaign of supposedly disconnected skullduggery will end up forming the season’s overall plot.

These things are all expected; the show’s conformation to its formula, lifted happily from Mick Herron’s bestselling Slough House books, is part of the pleasure of returning to one of Apple TV’s most satisfying shows year in and out. The differences between premieres mostly come down to tone, then, and Christ, but does the show’s sixth-season starter set a bleak one.

## Why This Matters

Even before we get to the River of it all—in what’s promising to be a pretty River-heavy season—we start with a blast from the dismal past: Paul Higgins’ Struan Loy, who returns after four seasons away to a) remind us of what an irritating, delusional git he could be, and b) burn to death in the most horrific manner possible.

The entertainment world moves fast, and this is a prime example.
