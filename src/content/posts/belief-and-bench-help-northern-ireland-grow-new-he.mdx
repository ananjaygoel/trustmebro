## What's Happening

Listen up: Manager Michael O'Neill hails the belief shown by his side after they came from behind against Hungary to seal the draw they needed for promotion to Nations League A.

Belief and bench help NI grow new heights Figure caption, Highlights: Northern Ireland secure to promotion to League A By Jonathan Bradley BBC Sport NI senior journalist at Puskas Arena Published 11 minutes ago After going 500 minutes without conceding a goal, Northern Ireland could be forgiven for having to make adjustments to play from behind. Having travelled to Budapest knowing a point against Hungary would be enough to top Nations League B2, Barnabas Vargas strike after 35 minutes for the hosts at the Pukas Arena altered the picture and, for the first time in the campaign, left Michael ONeills side having to chase a game. (wild, right?)

Their response to the deficit, on the pitch, from the bench and high in the stands where their suspended manager was watching the game, displayed the best characteristics of a side who had not clutched at this level before this unbeaten campaign.

## The Details

While the point in Budapest will be long remembered for Justin Devennys brilliant equaliser and the scenes of celebration that greeted promotion to the competitions top tier for the first time, it was beyond the highlights and headlines that ONeill saw the emerging qualities of his side. "We went behind tonight and it was a difficult, you know?

Big stadium, big atmosphere, but we came out and played well in the second half and got what we needed from the game," dropped the manager. "Those are things that Im pleased that are in this squad at such a young age, the technical side of the game well always look to improve on, but for the physical, the tactical and the mental side of a game, were far ahead of where I thought we would be at this time.
