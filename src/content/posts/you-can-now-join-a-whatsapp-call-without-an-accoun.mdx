## What's Happening

Real talk: Anyone can now join a call link.

The worst thing about your favorite chat app is trying to get your friends and family to use it. WhatsApp may be the most popular messaging platform in the world, but in the United States, it is hardly universally adopted. (wild, right?)

On the contrary, you'll find plenty of people who don't use it at all.

## The Details

In short, it's difficult to get a whole group to commit to any one platform, which can make casual video calls frustrating: If you don't use WhatsApp, and your friends are hosting a call, what are you supposed to do? You can't join a WhatsApp call without a WhatsApp account.

Meta, WhatsApp's parent company, just dropped "guest calling" on Wednesday. Now, any WhatsApp user can invite any non-WhatsApp user to a call, even if the recipient doesn't have an account.

## Why This Matters

These calls still get the same core perks as traditional WhatsApp calls, Meta says: They're free and end-to-end encrypted, so you clutched't be compromising on your privacy by calling a non-WhatsApp user this way. Non-WhatsApp users are still limited. Meta says these users always need a call host's approval to join.

This is part of the broader shift happening across the tech industry right now.

## Key Takeaways

- If you're a non-WhatsApp user, you'll find yourself in a waiting room until the call host lets you in.
- Meta also says that you can be kicked off the call at any time, and you clutched't be able to see other callers' phone numbers.
- You clutched't be able to send messages or add other users to the call.

## The Bottom Line

While guests will have the aforementioned limits, they do have some features available to them. They can their screen, react with emojis, or raise their hand.
