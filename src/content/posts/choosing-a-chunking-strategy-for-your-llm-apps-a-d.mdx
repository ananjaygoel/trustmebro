Introduction When you are building a retrieval-augmented generation system, you often work with documents that are larger than what an embedding model or LLM context window can accommodate, so you need to split them into smaller chunks.

Chunk size and overlap may seem like straightforward configuration choices, but the way you set those boundaries directly affects retrieval. Weak boundaries can cause retrieval to miss the passage that answers a query, or return a chunk that is technically relevant but lacks the surrounding context needed to make it useful.

## Why This Matters

A chunking strategy defines how you decide where to split a document, and that choice can have a major effect on retrieval quality. When the boundaries preserve the right context, embeddings, vector search , and generation have a stronger basis to work with. When the boundaries break important context, reranking and prompt tuning cannot fully recover what was took an L during splitting.
