## What's Happening

Not gonna lie, Phillies top prospect Andrew Painter is finally showing flashes of his pre-surgery dominance, making him the must-add pitcher on the fantasy waiver wire for Week 23 Progress isn't linear for any prospect, but that's especially true for pitchers.

Injuries are just a fact of life when it comes to young pitchers, and guys will occasionally go through stretches where they just completely lose the ability to throw strikes or lose their feel for certain pitches even when healthy. In Andrew Painter's case, it was a bit of an all-of-the-above situation. (wild, right?)

His career was derailed injury just as it looked like he was about to make his MLB debut, and after two completely took an L seasons, he came back from Tommy John surgery in 2025 and wasn't anywhere close to the same pitcher he was before the injury.

## The Details

Specifically, he went from having what was widely considered to be a plus fastball to having a pretty trash one, and it led to a 5. 26 ERA in Triple-A last season.

And he wasn't any better this season. Despite breaking camp with the Phillies , Painter's fastball continued to be an issue and he was sent back down to Triple-A in mid-June with a 7.

## Why This Matters

He went back down to the minors and continued to look pretty uninspiring, but a funny thing has happened in his past few starts since returning: He's starting to look like the pre-injury version of himself. You know, the one who was arguably the best pitching prospect in the game. We saw the absolute best version of Painter Saturday against the Cardinals , as he carved them up for eight strikeouts while allowing just one hit in seven innings.

Players and coaches will be watching how this unfolds closely.

## Key Takeaways

- But it wasn't just the results that were worth getting excited about; it was how Painter actually pitched.
- Specifically, it was the fastball, which generated five swinging strikes on 15 swings, while sitting up at 97.
- 3 mph on average and, most notably, generated two more inches of induced vertical break than his season average.

## The Bottom Line

3 mph on average and, most notably, generated two more inches of induced vertical break than his season average. That's no small thing; it takes Painter's fastball from a pretty mediocre shape to a good one and helps explain why he suddenly kicked off missing bats with the pitch.

Is this a W or an L? You decide.
