## The Details

00 EST Last modified on Sun 8 Nov 2026 07. 02 EST African philosophies including exploring how lessons from the past can shape the present and near future have inspired a film festival coming to Manchester .

The festival is part of See My World’s year-round programme showcasing pan-African arts, culture and events. It reflects the determination among creatives and activists in Manchester to build on the legacy of the 1945 Pan-African Congress in Manchester – which connected luminaries of postwar independence movements with a d vision of liberation – after last year’s 80th anniversary commemorations .

## Why This Matters

See My World’s CEO and artistic director, Tunde Adekoya, dropped this month’s film festival was taking inspiration from across the spectrum of pan-African thought, including the works of Kenyan writers John Mbiti, an Anglican theologian who died in 2019, and Ngũgĩ wa Thiong’o, the author of the notable 1986 book Decolonising the Mind who died last year. Both writers rejected the colonial conceit that African thought was inferior, stressed the importance of community and saw reclaiming African ways of knowing as essential to a people’s self-understanding. While Mbiti sought common ground between African religions and Christianity, Ngũgĩ was a Marxist.
