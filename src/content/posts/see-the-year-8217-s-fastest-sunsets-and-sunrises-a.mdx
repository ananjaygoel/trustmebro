## The Details

Thats 19:05 CDT on September 22. And heres a little-known equinox phenomenon: the sun sets faster around the time of an equinox.

The fastest sunrises happen at or near the equinoxes, too. On the other hand, the slowest sunsets (and sunrises) happen around the solstices.

## Why This Matters

Its true whether you live in Earths Northern or Southern Hemisphere. , when we say sunset here, were talking about the actual number of minutes it takes for the body of the sun to sink below the western horizon. Why does the body of the sun fall below the horizon so quickly at equinox time?

The scientific community tends to find developments like this significant.

## Key Takeaways

- Its because, at every equinox, the sun rises due east and sets due west.
- That means on the day of an equinox the setting sun hits the horizon at its steepest possible angle.
- Meanwhile, at a solstice, the sun is setting farthest north or farthest south of due west.
- And, the farther the sun sets from due west along the horizon, the shallower the angle of the setting sun.
