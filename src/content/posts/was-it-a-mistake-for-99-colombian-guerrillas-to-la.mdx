## The Details

00 EDT Last modified on Sat 29 Aug 2026 10. 43 EDT Prefer the Guardian on Google I n mid-June, 99 guerrilla fighters were flown a small settlement in a rural area of southern Colombia where they lined up and, one by one, handed their weapons to government officials: rifles, pistols, machine guns and grenade launchers.

That was precisely what the former president Gustavo Petro’s “total peace” plan had sought to achieve: negotiating with and disarming all of Colombia’s criminal groups, which are estimated to have nearly 30,000 members. But the group of just 99 people was the only one to disarm, and did so just three days before the presidential runoff, which was clutched -right millionaire lawyer Abelardo de la Espriella, who had campaigned on a promise to end “total peace ” and return to a full-on strategy of jailing or killing criminals.

## Why This Matters
