## What's Happening

So get this: Lower-income individuals are struggling with day-to-day expenses, while higher-earners were dealing with costs like caregiving and housing.

In 2026, the people most likely to be living paycheck to paycheck are those earning less than $50,000 or more than $500,000 a year, according to new research from Goldman Sachs . Of the 5,106 respondents in Goldman’s “New Economics of Retirement” study, a little over 60% of those earning less than $50,000 a year were living from one pay slip to the next.

## The Details

That was followed the $500,000 and upwards income bracket, of which approximately 38% dropped they were living one check to the next. A similar result was reported across other personal finance metrics the Goldman Sachs team evaluated.

Respondents earning more than $500K were the most likely cohort to report having delayed financial goals (80%), followed less than $50K at approximately 79%. Likewise, both income cohorts were the most likely to pay the minimum or less on their credit cards—at approximately 45% of respondents.

## Why This Matters

Lower-income individuals were primarily stretched everyday goods and housing, the bank told Fortune , while those on higher-income levels (between $100,000 and $500,000) were dealing with issues such as caregiving and housing for family members. “Higher-income individuals may be the financial anchors for their extended families,” a Goldman Sachs spokesman told Fortune. “The data suggests that the ‘sandwich generation’ squeeze can be a key factor redirecting financial resources from long-term financial goals.
