From the pedigree behind it (Shuyu Li and Wenyu Liu are the dynamic duo of China’s indie 2D animation movement and the co-founders of Flint Sugar studio) to the promise of an introspective coming-of-age story wrapped in surreal science fiction, False Memory already feels like one of the most intriguing anime on the near horizon.

## The Details

But while this story of a young boy who stumbles upon a surreal universe using a time-altering device looks to be every bit promising in its delivery, as a long-time anime fan, I can’t help noticing similarities it s with another series that's well over two decades old. These two shows somehow seem to speak the same language.

Both portray adolescence as something messy, confusing, and almost impossible to explain with ordinary reality. The only way to capture growing up is through tower-sized metaphors, impossible technology, or visitors from somewhere beyond our understanding.

## Why This Matters

Long before False Memory existed, a six-episode series by Gainax (the famed anime studio behind Neon Genesis Evangelion , Gurren Lagann , and Gunbuster ) perfected this idea with one of the most defining OVAs in existence. It's difficult to overstate just how singular FLCL (short for Furikuri, or "Foolycooly" in English) felt in 2000.
