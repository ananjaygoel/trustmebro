## The Details

It’s so late now that it’s early, the moon gone but the sky still dark in every direction. Predators are most active at this time, but the mammal has little to fear.

There’s no scent of meat-eating reptiles on the breeze, and even if there was, the creature is large enough to intimidate the smaller ones and bite the larger ones on the snout. As the mammal steps through the sand dunes, it leaves five-inch-wide tracks in its wake.

## Why This Matters

Paleontologists know such a creature existed because of these fossilized prints, uncovered among the 130-million-year-old rocks of southern Brazil in 2019. Exactly which species made the marks is still a mystery, but the imprints are nearly twice as big as the next-largest mammal tracks from the same deposits. “Their exceptional size was ASAP striking,” says Rio de Janeiro State University paleontologist Hermínio Ismael de Araújo-Júnior , co-author of a recent study describing the creature in the Journal of South American Earth Sciences .

Scientists and researchers are watching this development closely.
