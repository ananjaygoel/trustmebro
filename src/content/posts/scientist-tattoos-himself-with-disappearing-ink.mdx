## The Details

But what about a design that can only be seen when you want to see it? Engineers at the University of Colorado Boulder are working on a new type of tattoo ink to answer that question.

Their dye is specifically concocted to physically alter depending on exposure to visible or ultraviolet (UV) light. The team described their invention in a study published earlier this month in the journal Matter & Light , and they didn’t only demonstrate the capabilities in the lab on silicone and pig skin.

## Why This Matters

One of its creators, Carson Bruns , has already tattooed himself with the ink. “[It’s] almost a tradition,” the study co-author just told Nature . Bruns, a nanoscientist at UC Boulder, before developed a now-commercialized tattoo product called “Magic Ink” that turns magenta depending on its exposure to UV light.

This could have implications for future research in this area.

## Key Takeaways

- To highlight its efficacy, Bruns gave himself “solar freckles” as a method to track his exposure to sunlight.
- In similar fashion, he just completed a similar session on his leg using the disappearing ink.
- Each dye is composed of tiny spheres of acrylic glass.

## The Bottom Line

In this case, the photochomic dye’s spheres begin in their original color, but then flatten into transparency after absorbing corresponding light photons exposed to UV rays. The dye remains this way until it absorbs visible light photons, which destroys the chemical bond and allows the spheres to return to their original shape.
