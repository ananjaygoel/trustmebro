## The Details

Norris’ play “Downstate” opened Sunday at Booth Theatre after productions at Off Broadway’s Playwrights Horizons, Chicago’s Steppenwolf and London’s National Theatre. “Downstate” takes place in a church-run residence for sex offenders who live in a house that is continually being vandalized.

Those concerned (and often violent) neighbors have also joined forces to expand the safety-zone perimeter around a local school that will have the effect of restricting even further the mobility, not to mention the grocery-shopping habits, of these four men convicted of sex crimes against minors. The warning about the new law comes courtesy of Ivy (Liza Colon-Zayas), a case worker who shows little sympathy for her charges.

## Why This Matters

Shes just one woman against four men, but Ivy has the law on her side and wears it like an AR-45 bulletproof body armor. Norris also uses Ivy’s status in the legal system to toy with our sympathies. As precise as a metronome, this playwright’s exposition drops one fact after another regarding just how little freedom these men retain after serving considerable prison sentences.
