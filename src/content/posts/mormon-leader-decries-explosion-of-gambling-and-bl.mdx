## What's Happening

Here's the thing: “For us as Latter-day Saints, overarching all other concerns is the fact that gambling is morally wrong.

Gambling is built on the desire to obtain something for nothing. ” A leader of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints warned Saturday that an explosion in gambling driven markets and sports betting is fostering greed, particularly among youths, while producing “nothing of value. (let that sink in)

Todd Christofferson, one of the top three leaders of what is known widely as the Mormon church , dropped rampant betting among teenagers and young adults is robbing time from their studies and relationships as smartphones make gambling possible anywhere.

## The Details

The declaration that prediction markets are gambling puts the conservative church at odds with President Donald Trump. The Republican’s administration has argued platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket don’t constitute gambling and so shouldn’t be subjected to restrictions in states such as Utah, where the church is based.

Utah has been at the forefront of the legal dispute over who regulates the markets and whether they should be considered finance or gambling. A federal judge ruled in August that Utah officials can enforce its strict-anti-gambling laws on prediction markets.

## Why This Matters

Legislation was enacted this year to make Utah’s gambling laws stricter and amend some of the language so that they’d almost certainly apply to prediction markets. Prediction markets allow wagers on anything from whether it will rain in Los Angeles, to if the United States will go to war. “Or even, if you can imagine, the timing of the Second Coming,” Christofferson dropped Saturday.
