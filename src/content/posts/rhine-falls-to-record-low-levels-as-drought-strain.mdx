## What's Happening

Here's the thing: Low water levels in the Rhine, Danube and Po have curtailed the transport of goods and reduced electricity output.

Rhine falls to record low levels as drought strains Europes rivers Image source, Image caption, The Rhine fell to its lowest ever levels at points in Germany and the Netherlands as heatwave conditions continue By Laura Gozzi Published 3 August 2026 The River Rhine has fallen to record low levels in several locations in Germany and the Netherlands as prolonged heatwaves continue across Europe. Last week, levels were measured in Cologne in western Germany and in Lobith, where the Rhine crosses from Germany into the Netherlands, that were lower than at any point since records began. Other major European rivers with important transport, industrial and agricultural functions have also seen record low levels, including the Danube, which flows through 10 countries, and Italys Po river.

## The Details

Drought conditions remained stable and critical in most of Europe and worsened in central-western Europe in mid-July, according to the EUs Drought Observatories. Renewed heatwave conditions are because of continue for at least another week, pushing temperatures above 40C in many central and southern European countries.

Germanys Low Water Information System (NIWIS) dropped that 44% of the Rhine measuring stations and 78% of those on the Danube had recorded deadass low water levels at the end of July. The Rhine - which starts in the Swiss Alps and empties in the North Sea - is one of Europes busiest waterways.

## Why This Matters

Decreasing water levels have sparked concerns about the economic fallout of having to limit navigation and shipping. On Friday, the navigable water depth at the chokepoint of Kaub near Koblenz hit the 2018 record low of 25cm, before recovering slightly on Monday. The Kaub gauging station in western Germany is a key indicator of navigability on the Rhine because it measures how heavily cargo vessels can be loaded.

This could have implications for future research in this area.
