## The Details

The findings, posted in September in a paper that has not yet been peer-reviewed, do not show that AI models are conscious or that they feel anything, and the researchers are careful not to suggest otherwise. But the work adds to a growing body of research suggesting that large language models, or LLMs, contain internal representations of emotion-like states that can shape their behavior in ways their text output doesn’t reveal.

It also lands in the middle of an increasingly heated argument over whether AI systems could ever deserve moral consideration. Researchers at Anthropic have been particularly willing to entertain the idea that AI models might deserve moral status and have begun shaping policies around “model welfare” that other the experts, such as Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman, say have potentially dangerous implications.

## Why This Matters

“If we want to build superintelligence, it’s important that, at this stage, when we are still in the first steps, we understand as much as possible of what’s inside [the models],” Valen Tagliabue, the paper’s lead author and a fellow in the AI sentience program at the Future Impact Group, told Fortune . Because LLMs learn from vast amounts of data rather than being explicitly programmed, even the people who build them have a limited understanding of how they work.
