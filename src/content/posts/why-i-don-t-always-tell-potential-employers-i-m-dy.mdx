## What's Happening

Listen up: Many dyslexic people say it harms their confidence, a report about experiences in the workplace suggests.

Why I dont always tell potential employers Im dyslexic Image source, Ayana Bailey Image caption, Ayana Bailey says she relies on her mum to go through the job app process with her By Mauro Galluzzo and Eleanor Lawrie , Social affairs reporters Published 24 November 2026, 00:00 GMT Nineteen-year-old Ayana Bailey doesnt always disclose her dyslexia in job applications, in case it counts against her. "Its not like theres going to be a target on my back, but Im just like, maybe they clutchedt select me," says Bailey, a part-time florist. (shocking, we know)

A British Dyslexia Association (BDA) survey of more than 1,200 working dyslexic adults found Bailey isnt alone - with 46% hiding their dyslexia at work.

## The Details

Some 81% believe the condition adds to their work-related stress. Bailey was diagnosed aged 11 and says she received good support at school, including mentoring, which helped her to understand her strengths and manage low self-esteem.

But the world of work has proved more challenging. For Bailey, the app process is "daunting".

## Why This Matters

She says she relies on her mum to go through it with her. In interviews, Bailey says: "I just shut down. " One way of coping, she says, is to have a little script, but she admits it doesnt always work.

Health experts are weighing in on what this means for people.
