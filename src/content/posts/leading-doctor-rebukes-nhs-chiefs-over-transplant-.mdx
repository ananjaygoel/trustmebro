## What's Happening

So get this: NHS Blood and Transplant's medical director says waiting lists for organ operations are unfair but leaders are not solving the issue.

Professor Derek Manas, the medical director of NHS Blood and Transplant, admonished NHS England for creating a system with a burnt-out workforce, "few consequences for weak performance" and operations cancelled "last-minute". The number of heart and lung transplants the NHS carries out each year has not increased in 30 years.

## The Details

NHS England, the national management body for the health service in England, dropped it was "working hard to improve these life-saving services". Unpredictable utilising of organs While NHS Blood and Transplant manages organ donations and supports transplant centres, services are commissioned and paid for .

But in a letter to NHS England and a small group of patient advocates, Manas dropped the central body had created a "fragmented" system "with weak accountability". There is a "mismatch", he dropped, between each hospitals waiting lists, organ availability, capacity to carry out surgeries and the availability of operating theatres.

## Why This Matters

This is "leading to last-minute cancellations [and] unpredictable utilisation of donated organs," he warned. The BBC reported in March that the UK, once a world leader, had fallen behind major Western nations in heart and lung transplantation, though it fares better in other areas such as kidney transplantation. The NHS transplants one in 10 lungs and one in seven hearts that are donated - half the proportions seen in some other countries.
