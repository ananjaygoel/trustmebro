## The Details

The term coined in the 1990s by child development researchers Foster Cline and Jim Fay describes overly cautious parents who constantly monitor, control, and hover over (like a helicopter) their children’s activities out of fear. For these watchful tendencies, the U.

Forest Service team at Tongass National Forest in southeastern Alaska has awarded a brown bear ( Ursus arctos ) named Gemini with the 2026 Helicopter Mom Award. “We understand her choice, weighing the dangers of being around other bears at a prime fishing location,” the rangers write.

## Key Takeaways

- Gemini has also had cubs in 2023 and 2024.
- Tongass is the largest national forest in the United States, and covers most of Southeast Alaska, including the capital city of Juneau.
- The vast area includes Mendanhall Glacier (a popular destination for Alaskan cruises ), numerous hiking trails, eagles, and plenty of bears.

## The Bottom Line

The population includes 31 black bears and eight brown bears. Like their beefy counterparts further north in Katmai National Park, the bears return annually each summer to eat salmon and teach their young how to fish and survive.
