## The Details

The shells’ many shapes also reflect how different species have evolved over the 500 million years since mollusks first appeared. Back then, their shells were simple caps and coils.

Today’s mollusks include fanlike scallops, spiny conchs and spiraled nautiluses. Yet most people who pick up shells on the beach don’t give much thought to the animals that made them.

## Why This Matters

“The average citizen will say, ‘Well, I thought they were just there,’” says José Leal, the science director and curator at the Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum , on Sanibel Island, Florida. Leal reminds museumgoers that shells aren’t simply formed the way rocks are. A mollusk absorbs calcium and carbon from the water and fuses the molecules together, generating a rocklike material out of a body part called a mantle.

Scientists and researchers are watching this development closely.
