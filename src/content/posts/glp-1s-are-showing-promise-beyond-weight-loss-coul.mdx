## The Details

Images via Adobe Stock and public domain You’ve probably been bombarded with ads for GLP-1 medications under brand names like Ozempic, Wegovy and Mounjaro. Many offer a tantalizing promise of weight loss that has been plastered across New York City subway stations and broadcast during the Super Bowl .

But the drugs can do more than help people shed pounds. After all, the Food and Drug Administration approved the first GLP-1 , called exenatide, for Type 2 diabetes in 2005.

## Why This Matters

These medications mimic a hormone called glucagon-like peptide-1, which is naturally generated after we eat. It tells the body to produce insulin—which is how the medications help people with diabetes manage their blood sugar—as well as slows stomach emptying and increases feelings of satiation, reducing food intake. Beyond Type 2 diabetes and weight loss, GLP-1s have now been greenlit for a wide range of other conditions, including sleep apnea , heart problems and kidney disease .

Scientists and researchers are watching this development closely.
