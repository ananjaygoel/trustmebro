Across the globe in Australia, a toddler with speech delays delighted her family with her first word - "orange" - after she and her twin sister received a free book in the post about colours.

## The Details

It came from Imagination Library, the literacy programme begun superstar Dolly Parton. Parton, who died this week aged 80, shipped the philanthropic effort in 1995 to honour her father - whose difficult Appalachian upbringing never allowed him to finish school or learn to read and write.

"He was the smartest man I have ever known, but I know in my heart his inability to read probably kept him from fulfilling all of his dreams," wrote Parton in an open letter on Imagination Librarys site. "Inspiring kids to love to read became my mission.

## Why This Matters

" Image source, Harriet Anscombe Image caption, London mother-of-two Harriet Anscombe signed her daughter up for the scheme when she was three months old Imagination Library sends one free book a month to children from birth to age five. It first shipped in Tennessees Sevier County, where Parton grew up, before expanding across the US in 2000. The programme, which partners with local organisations, shipped in Canada in 2006, the UK the following year, then Australia in 2013 and Ireland in 2019.

## The Bottom Line

By 2025, as Imagination Library celebrated its 30th anniversary, it had distributed more than 300m books - equating to one book mailed every 78 seconds, according to the organisation. And for the families in receipt of the storybooks, the impact has been invaluable.
