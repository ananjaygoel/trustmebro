## The Details

Whether it’s the belly, armpits, or feet, many people are ticklish at least somewhere on their body. Still, scientists have paid little attention to the silly sensation .

As such, two researchers took it upon themselves to investigate the experience, and found out that it is no laughing matter. Ziliang Xiong and Konstantina Kilteni , from the neuroscience department at the Karolinska Institute in Sweden, investigated the experience of getting tickled as well as the distribution of ticklish body parts in Chinese, Dutch, and Greek study participants.

## Why This Matters

In their study published in the journal Nature Human Behavior , Xiong and Kilteni tickled 448 adults…just kidding. They studied survey responses from 448 adults. Half of the participants were women, and they were all between the ages of 18 and 30.

Scientists and researchers are watching this development closely.

## Key Takeaways

- Seventy-one percent of the surveyed individuals described themselves as moderately to ticklish, 98.
- 4 percent reported having been tickled at least once, and 95 percent reported that they had tickled someone else.

## The Bottom Line

“Despite differences in cultural background, participants reported remarkably similar experiences of tickling, including its social dynamics, emotional responses, and how they evaluated the experience,” Xiong and Kilteni tell Popular Science . They didn’t find this result especially surprising “because tickling is such a common form of playful interaction, especially between parents and children,” they add.
