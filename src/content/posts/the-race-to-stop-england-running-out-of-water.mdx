## What's Happening

The race to stop England running out of water Published 29 August 2026, 01:46 BST Updated 1 hour ago By Esme Stallard , Climate and Science senior journalist and Miho Tanaka , Climate and Science senior journalist One evening in early August, farmer Daniel Sizers phone buzzed with a message. It was from his local water board and it read: "River levels are low but have recovered enough to allow one night of irrigation tonight. (yes, really)

" Like much of the lower half of England, Daniels fourth-generation farm had seen barely any rain in nearly three months.

## The Details

He shows us what is left of the peas and wheat crop at his 300-hectare site in Suffolk. "Theres no water in the river so half my crop is completely and utterly ruined.

" With river levels falling so low that taking any more water out of them risked endangering wildlife, in July the Environment Agency (EA) temporarily stopped Daniel and thousands of other farmers from watering their crops from the river. Image caption, Daniels farm has fared poorly in the drought England has long been used to hosepipe bans over the summer.

## Why This Matters

But what Daniel has experienced points to something newer and more worrying. The kind of increasingly frequent heatwaves we have experienced and greatly reduced rainfall that we could see over successive summers with climate change, plus a projected increase in demand for water, have raised the prospect that England could at some point run out of water. The result could be that by 2055 England will face a shortfall of five billion litres in public water supply a day, almost enough to fill Wembley Stadium four and a half times daily.

This could have implications for future research in this area.
