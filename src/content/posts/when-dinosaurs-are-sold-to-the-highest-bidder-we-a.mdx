## Why This Matters

They are irreplaceable snapshots of our planet's history and our own human journey. When scientifically important fossils are reduced to luxury commodities, we are robbed of the evidence that makes them valuable in the first place. 6-foot-long (6 meters) skeleton of Nanotyrannus lancensis known as "Jodi" was sold at Sotheby's in New York for $47,255,000, far exceeding its $6 million to $9 million estimate.

This could have implications for future research in this area.
