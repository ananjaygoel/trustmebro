Wildfires in the Amazon have consequences that go far beyond the destruction of vegetation, scientists say.

## The Details

A new study has spilled that ozone produced during these events also harms trees that survive the flames, damaging their leaves and making it harder for them to absorb carbon dioxide (CO2). Though its less visible than the direct damage caused by fire, this effect poses a real threat.

It represents an additional loss for the rainforest’s carbon balance and can become more severe during periods of intense drought. Researchers from the University of Exeter, ETH Zurich and James Cook University published their peer-reviewed study in the journal Communications Earth & Environment on .

## Why This Matters

Almost all fires in the Amazon are kicked off by people, mainly to clear land for agriculture and cattle ranching. But, these fires can get out of control and spread into surrounding areas of rainforest, destroying vast stretches of woodland. Wildfires release carbon dioxide into the atmosphere.

This could have implications for future research in this area.

## Key Takeaways

- And they also produce pollutants that contribute to the formation of whats called tropospheric ozone.
- Ozone is also found in the stratosphere, which is the atmospheric layer above the troposphere.
- In the stratosphere, ozone helps protect us from much of the sun’s ultraviolet radiation.

## The Bottom Line

In this way, wildfires not only release carbon , but also make it harder for the surviving rainforest to absorb some of that carbon. Tropospheric ozone pollution damages tree leaves and reduces the efficiency of photosynthesis, the process through which trees absorb carbon dioxide.
