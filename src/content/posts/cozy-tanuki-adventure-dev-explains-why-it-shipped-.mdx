Most studios wouldn't pick the week between The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time and GTA 6 to launch their debut game, but Denkiworks did. Its cozy adventure, Tanuki: Pon's Summer , arrived on November 10, and the three-person studio says there were good reasons to take the risk. (shocking, we know)

## The Details

By Tayn Garcia The Kyoto studio's debut follows Pon , a tanuki delivering packages restoring a shrine before a summer festival. It's a different game from Rockstar 's crime epic, but that doesn't make finding an audience in November any easier.

In an interview with GamesIndustry. Biz , Denkiworks co-founder and game director Liam Edwards broke down why the team stuck with November and what it hopes happens once players have had their fill of the bigger games.

## Why This Matters

Why November Made Sense for Denkiworks The date wasn't chosen to challenge Nintendo or Rockstar . Denkiworks first aimed for summer but needed longer to finish the game and prepare a multiplatform release. October became the next target, until platform marketing opportunities made November more attractive, Edwards broke down.

## The Bottom Line

Denkiworks wanted the game available across PC and consoles at launch, which is a lot to ask of a three-person core team. With help from its publisher, an external porting team handled the Xbox , original Switch , and PlayStation versions, while Denkiworks concentrated on PC and Switch 2 .
